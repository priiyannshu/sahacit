UPSC Civil Services: Royal Lineages & Dynastic Chronology

High-Yield History Compendium for UPSC CSE Prelims & Mains (GS Paper I & History Optional)

1. Ancient India: Magadha to Guptas & Harsha

Magadhan Dynasties (6th–4th Century BCE) Capital: Rajgriha / Girivraja → Pataliputra
Bimbisara → Ajatashatru → Udayin → Shishunaga → Kalashoka (Kakavarna) → Mahapadma Nanda → Dhana Nanda
  • Bimbisara Sent royal physician Jivaka to Avanti king Pradyota; forged matrimonial alliances with Kosala (Prasenjit's sister), Lichchhavis (Chellana), and Madra clan.
  • Ajatashatru Convened the First Buddhist Council at Rajgriha (Sattapani cave) under Mahakassapa; deployed military machines Mahashilakantaka (catapult) and Rathamusala (chariot with blades).
  • Udayin Founded the imperial capital Pataliputra at the sacred confluence of the Ganga and Son rivers.
  • Kalashoka Convened the Second Buddhist Council at Vaishali (led to Sthaviravada vs Mahasanghika split).
  • Mahapadma Nanda Assumed sovereign titles Ekarat and Sarvakshatrantaka; mentioned in Kharavela’s Hathigumpha inscription. Overthrown by Chandragupta Maurya.
Mauryan Empire (c. 322–185 BCE) Emblem: Lion Capital | Capital: Pataliputra
Chandragupta Maurya → Bindusara → Ashoka the Great → Dasharatha / Samprati → Brihadratha (Last)
  • Chandragupta Maurya Guided by Chanakya (Kautilya's Arthashastra); defeated Seleucus I Nicator (gained Aria, Arachosia, Gedrosia, Paropamisadae); hosted Megasthenes (Indica); Jain Sallekhana at Shravanabelagola.
  • Bindusara Title Amitraghata (Slayer of foes); patron of Ajivika sect; hosted Greek ambassador Deimachus sent by Antiochus I.
  • Ashoka Kalinga War (261 BCE; MRE XIII); converted to Buddhism under Upagupta; organized 3rd Buddhist Council at Pataliputra (Moggaliputta Tissa); promulgated Dhamma edicts across Brahmi, Kharosthi, Greek, and Aramaic.
  • Brihadratha Assassinated in 185 BCE by his Brahmin commander-in-chief (*Senapati*) Pushyamitra Shunga during a military parade.
Satavahana Dynasty of Deccan (c. 1st C. BCE – 3rd C. CE) Capitals: Pratishthana (Paithan) & Amaravati
Simuka → Satakarni I → Hala → Gautamiputra Satakarni → Vashishtiputra Pulumayi → Yajna Sri Satakarni
  • Administrative First Earliest Indian kingdom to issue royal land grants (Brahmadeya) with tax immunities to Buddhist monks and Brahmins.
  • Gautamiputra Satakarni Nasik Cave Prashasti by mother Gautami Balashri; styled Ekabrahmana; crushed Western Saka ruler Nahapana (restruck Jogalthambi hoard coins).
  • Hala Composed the celebrated Prakrit poetry anthology Gatha Saptasati (Sattasai).
  • Coinage & Trade Issued lead, potin, and copper coins; Vashishtiputra Pulumayi and Yajna Sri Satakarni minted coins featuring two-masted ships, symbolizing maritime power.
Imperial Gupta Dynasty (c. 319–550 CE) Capital: Pataliputra & Ujjain (Secondary)
Sri Gupta → Ghatotkacha → Chandragupta I → Samudragupta → Chandragupta II 'Vikramaditya' → Kumaragupta I → Skandagupta
  • Chandragupta I Assumed title Maharajadhiraja; initiated Gupta Era (319/320 CE); struck King-and-Queen gold coins with Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi.
  • Samudragupta Prayag Prashasti composed in chaste Sanskrit Champu style by court poet Harishena; distinguished policies for Aryavarta (*Prasabhoddharana*) and Dakshinapatha (*Grahana-Moksha-Anugraha*); struck lyrist (veena) and Asvamedha coins.
  • Chandragupta II Exterminated Western Saka Kshatrapas; Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription (King 'Chandra'); court graced by Navaratnas (Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Dhanvantari, Varahamihira); visited by Chinese pilgrim Fa-Hien.
  • Kumaragupta I Founded the renowned Nalanda Mahavihara; issued peacock-type gold coins; issued highest number of Gupta inscriptions.
  • Skandagupta Bhitari pillar inscription records repulse of White Huna invaders; Junagadh inscription details restoration of the Sudarshana Lake dam by his governor Parnadatta and son Chakrapalita.

Rashtrakutas of Manyakheta (c. 753–973 CE) Capital: Manyakheta (Malkhed)
Dantidurga → Krishna I → Govinda III → Amoghavarsha I → Indra III → Krishna III
  • Dantidurga Overthrew Chalukya ruler Kirtivarman II in 753 CE; performed the sacred Hiranyagarbha (Golden Womb) ritual at Ujjain.
  • Krishna I Chiseled out the monolithic rock-cut architectural wonder Kailasa Temple at Ellora (Cave 16).
  • Amoghavarsha I Reign of 64 years; devout Jain disciple of Jinasena; authored Kavirajamarga (earliest landmark treatise in Kannada literature) and Prashnottara Ratnamalika in Sanskrit; praised by Arab traveler Sulaiman as one of the 4 greatest contemporary monarchs in the world.
  • Krishna III Decisively defeated Chola crown prince Rajaditya at the Battle of Takkolam (949 CE); erected a triumph pillar at Rameswaram.
Imperial Cholas of Thanjavur (c. 850–1279 CE) Capitals: Thanjavur & Gangaikondacholapuram
Vijayalaya → Aditya I → Parantaka I → Rajaraja I → Rajendra I → Rajadhiraja I → Kulottunga I
  • Parantaka I Captured Madurai from Pandyas (title Maduraikonda); famous Uttaramerur Inscriptions (919 and 921 CE) detailing autonomous rural governance, qualifying criteria, and Kudavolai (pot ballot system).
  • Rajaraja I (985–1014 CE) Erected the grand Brihadisvara Temple (Rajarajeshvara) at Thanjavur (1010 CE); initiated systematic land revenue surveys; annexed northern Sri Lanka (*Mummudi-Chola-mandalam*).
  • Rajendra I (1014–1044 CE) Marched to the banks of the Ganges (title Gangaikondachola) and founded Gangaikondacholapuram with the artificial lake Chola Gangam; executed successful naval expeditions across the Bay of Bengal against the Srivijaya Empire (Sumatra, Malay peninsula).
  • Kulottunga I United Eastern Chalukyan and Chola lines; abolished inland toll barriers (title Sungamtavirtta Cholan); sent large mercantile embassies to Song dynasty China.

Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646 CE) Four Dynasties: Sangama → Saluva → Tuluva → Aravidu
Harihara I & Bukka I (Sangama) → Deva Raya II → Saluva Narasimha (Saluva) → Krishnadevaraya (Tuluva) → Achyuta Deva Raya → Sadashiva Raya & Rama Raya → Tirumala (Aravidu)
  • Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529 CE) Golden epoch; authored magnum opus Amuktamalyada (Telugu treatise on statecraft) and Jambavati Kalyanam (Sanskrit drama); court adorned by Ashtadiggajas (Allasani Peddana, Tenali Ramakrishna); built Hazara Rama and Vijaya Vittala temples; hosted Portuguese chroniclers Domingo Paes and Duarte Barbosa.
  • Battle of Talikota (1565 CE) Also called Rakshasi-Tangadi; combined forces of Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, and Bidar crushed the Vijayanagara army under regent Aliya Rama Raya, leading to the sacking of Hampi.
  • Administrative Foundations Nayankara System (military governors holding amaram land) and Ayagar System (village council of 12 hereditary functionaries holding tax-free manya plots).

4. Mughal Empire & Maratha Swarajya

The Great Mughals & Sur Dynasty (1526–1707 CE) Imperial Architecture, Mansabdari, and Land Systems
Babur → Humayun → Sher Shah Suri (Sur Interregnum) → Akbar → Jahangir → Shah Jahan → Aurangzeb
  • Babur (1526–1530) Decisive battles: Panipat (1526, gun carriage Rumi / Tulghuma), Khanwa (1527 against Rana Sanga, declared Jihad), Chanderi (1528), Ghaghra (1529); penned autobiography Baburnama (Tuzuk-i-Baburi).
  • Sher Shah Suri (1540–1545) Introduced the silver Rupiya (178 grains) and copper Dam; constructed the Sadak-e-Azam (Grand Trunk Road) with spaced caravanserais (*Sarais*); unified revenue survey system (*Zabti* / *Ray*).
  • Akbar (1556–1605) Built imperial stability: Mansabdari System with dual dual rank decimal structure (*Zat* = personal status, *Sawar* = cavalry quota); Ain-i-Dahsala revenue assessment with Raja Todar Mal; founded Ibadat Khana (1575), proclaimed Mahzarnama (1579), and ethical code Sulh-i-Kul.
  • Jahangir (1605–1627) Golden age of Mughal miniature painting (court artists Ustad Mansur, Abul Hasan, Bishandas); installed the Golden Chain of Justice (Zanjir-i-Adl); hosted British ambassadors Captain William Hawkins and Sir Thomas Roe.
  • Shah Jahan (1628–1658) Zenith of marble architecture: Taj Mahal, Red Fort (Delhi), Jama Masjid, Peacock Throne; European travelers Francois Bernier and Jean-Baptiste Tavernier observed court life.
  • Aurangzeb Alamgir (1658–1707) Expanded boundaries to greatest extent; reimposed Jizya (1679); abolished court music and Jharokha Darshan; compiled legal digest Fatawa-i-Alamgiri; succumbed to protracted Deccan campaign (*Deccan Ulcer*).
