# Polity Spine — Phase 1 Coverage Analysis

*Snapshot date: 2026-04-29. After draft of all 11 chapters in `polity-narrative/`. Phase 1 = story-spine without nameplates. Phase 2 = nameplate addition (article numbers, body names, case names, amendment numbers) onto the homes already designed in.*

This report inventories what the eleven-chapter spine concretely narrates, what it omits, what Phase-2 dockings will close, and what genuinely needs supplementary surface-treatment beyond the spine. The purpose: once the user has internalised Phase 1, this document tells us what is still missing and where the gaps live.

---

## 1. Top-line estimate

- **By syllabus-topic: roughly 60-65% covered concretely.**
- **By PYQ-weight (frequency × marks): roughly 70-75% covered**, because the spine concentrates on heavily-tested conceptual chapters (FRs, DPSPs, Basic Structure, Federalism, Emergency, Reservations) and underweights procedurally-tested institutional minutiae (money bills, parliamentary committees, presidential ordinances).

Two caveats before reading further:

- *Concrete narration* here means the concept is named, structurally explained, and historically grounded — not just gestured at. Phase-1 vocabulary discipline (no article numbers, no body nameplates) is **not** a coverage gap; those nameplates dock onto pre-built homes in Phase 2.
- The spine is built around **eleven governance problems**, not around Laxmikanth's eighty institutional chapters. The mapping is genuine but lossy in both directions: some institutional chapters are fully absorbed into spine-questions; others have no natural home in the spine at all.

## 5. Structural gaps the spine genuinely doesn't address

These are topics where the **spine has no home for them** — they aren't covered now and won't be covered by Phase-2 dockings. They need either spine-extension chapters or supplementary surface-treatment outside the polity surface:

### 5.1 Citizenship (Articles 5-11, Citizenship Act 1955, OCI/PIO, NRC/CAA)

The spine treats *who consents* (Ch1) but never *who counts as the consenting* in legal-procedural detail. **High-PYQ-frequency topic.** Earlier design memo flagged this as borderline-Ch1-expansion. Could be added as Ch 1.5, or treated as a sidebar to Ch1.

### 5.2 President / PM / Governor / CM specific powers and discretion

The spine narrates the parliamentary fiction (head of state acts on aid+advice) but not the specific powers:

- President's executive, legislative, judicial, financial, emergency, ordinance, veto types (absolute, suspensive, pocket), pardoning power
- PM's specific functions: relationship with President, Cabinet appointment, council formation, dissolution recommendation
- Governor's discretionary powers: formation of government in hung assembly, dissolution, reservation of bills for President's assent, "pleasure" doctrine
- CM's relationship with Governor in scheduled areas

**Heavily PYQ-tested.** These need a separate "executive details" surface that hooks into Ch5.

### 5.3 Parliamentary procedure

- Money bills vs financial bills vs ordinary bills (Articles 109, 110, 117)
- Special majorities — simple, absolute, effective, special (different definitions for different procedures)
- Joint sittings (Article 108) — when convened, who presides
- Parliamentary committees: Departmentally Related Standing Committees (24); financial committees (PAC, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings); other committees (Privileges, Ethics, Business Advisory)
- Motions: no-confidence, censure, adjournment, calling attention, privilege, cut motions, financial motions
- Question Hour, Zero Hour, Half-an-hour discussion
- Privileges of Parliament and legislators (Article 105 / 194)

**Heavily PYQ-tested, possibly the largest gap by question-count.** Needs its own substantial supplementary chapter.

### 5.4 Specific quasi-judicial / executive bodies

- **NITI Aayog** (replaced Planning Commission 2015) — strategic shift from centralised planning to cooperative federalism
- **CBI** (Delhi Special Police Establishment Act 1946) — central investigative agency
- **NIA** (NIA Act 2008) — counter-terrorism
- **NDMA** (Disaster Management Act 2005)
- **State-level bodies**: SHRCs, SICs, Lokayuktas (variable across states)
- Inter-State Council and Zonal Councils functioning details

Some have homes (Ch10 absorbs the constitutional bodies cleanly), but specific functional details don't dock automatically.

### 5.5 Political parties and political culture

- Recognition of political parties (national, state, registered-unrecognised)
- Symbol order under EC
- Coalition arithmetic, common minimum programme
- Anti-defection mechanics beyond the schedule (Speaker's adjudication procedure, court review)
- Pressure groups, civil society
- Electoral funding (electoral bonds, corporate donations, expenditure limits)
- Criminalisation of politics (ADR data, RPA disqualifications, court rulings on convicted legislators)

The spine is institution-and-document-centric; it doesn't address the political *culture* that operates through the institutions.

### 5.6 RPA specifics

- RPA 1950 — preparation of electoral rolls, allocation of seats, delimitation
- RPA 1951 — qualifications, disqualifications, election offences, corrupt practices (s.123 listed eight categories), election petitions
- Model Code of Conduct
- Electoral reforms: NOTA, VVPAT, electronic voting, online enrolment, postal ballots

Ch7 names the Acts as "the legislation Parliament has made under its electoral powers" but doesn't itemise.

### 5.7 Comparison with other constitutions (sources borrowed)

Only Irish DPSPs (Ch3) and Weimar Article 48 (Ch8) are pulled in. Full Laxmikanth survey of borrowings is missing:

- **UK**: parliamentary system, rule of law, single citizenship, parliamentary privileges, writs, cabinet system, Speaker
- **US**: judicial review, written constitution, FRs, independence of judiciary, impeachment, removal of judges, post of VP
- **Canada**: federation with strong centre, residuary power at Centre, Governor-of-state-as-Centre's-representative, advisory jurisdiction of SC
- **Ireland**: DPSPs, nomination to Rajya Sabha, presidential election method
- **Australia**: concurrent list, freedom of trade and commerce, joint sitting
- **Japan**: procedure established by law (mistakenly attributed by some texts; actually procedure-clause was Japanese-influenced)
- **Weimar Germany**: emergency provisions
- **South Africa**: amendment procedure, election of Rajya Sabha members
- **France**: republic, ideals of liberty/equality/fraternity in Preamble
- **USSR**: Fundamental Duties, ideal of justice (social/economic/political) in Preamble

This is heavily PYQ-tested and needs a dedicated treatment.

### 5.8 Government's legal personality

Suits by/against government, Article 299/300 contracts, sovereign immunity. Niche, but PYQ-tested.

### 5.9 Other gaps

- **Salient features** as a topic — sometimes asked as standalone (e.g., "Which of the following are features of Indian Constitution?")
- **Right to Property history** — was FR (Articles 19(1)(f), 31), removed by 44th Amendment 1978, now Article 300A (legal right). Ch9 could absorb but doesn't currently.
- **NCRWC (Venkatachaliah Commission 2002)** — National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution, recommendations on parliamentary reform, federalism, FRs. PYQ-tested.

## 6. Strategic recommendations

### What to do after Phase 1 internalisation

1. **Run Phase-2 nameplate dockings.** Article numbers, body names, case names, amendment numbers all dock onto the existing prose without new substantive content. This raises coverage from ~65% to ~80% mechanically.

2. **Build a "procedure-and-mechanics" supplementary surface.** Phone-first, similar in design language to the spine, focused on *how* rather than *why*. Should cover §5.2 (executive specifics), §5.3 (parliamentary procedure), §5.4 (quasi-judicial bodies), §5.6 (RPA specifics).

3. **Add a "comparative origins" sidebar to the spine.** Covers §5.7 (sources borrowed). Could be a short standalone chapter prefixed to Ch1.

4. **Add Ch 1.5 on citizenship.** Or expand Ch1's scope to include who-counts-as-the-consenting.

5. **Address political culture in a separate surface.** §5.5 (parties, pressure groups, electoral funding, criminalisation) is more current-affairs-shaped than constitutional-architecture-shaped. May not belong in the polity surface at all; could live in current-affairs.

### What the spine is genuinely good at

- **Conceptual discrimination questions** ("Which of the following is correct about basic structure?") — ~70% of polity PYQs are this shape, and the spine prepares the user well.
- **Doctrinal arc questions** (Kesavananda → Minerva Mills → IR Coelho progression) — Ch9 narrates this completely.
- **Crisis-and-recovery questions** (1975 emergency, 44th Amendment, ADM Jabalpur dissent) — Ch8 is, in this domain, more thorough than most textbooks.
- **Federalism questions** (quasi-federal, residuary, fiscal asymmetry) — Ch6 prepares well.
- **Reservation questions** (genealogy, Mandal, creamy layer, asymmetric federalism) — Ch11 prepares well.

### What the spine is genuinely weak at

- **Itemised-recall questions** (Article matchings, Schedule contents, specific commission compositions) — ~30% of polity PYQs. Phase 2 closes about half of this; the rest needs supplementary recall-mode treatment.
- **Procedure questions** (money bill criteria, joint sitting conditions, types of motions) — barely touched.
- **Specific powers questions** (President's pardoning power, Governor's discretion, ordinance scope) — barely touched.

## 7. Bottom line

The spine **narrates the conceptual core of polity completely** — every governance problem, every doctrinal arc, every constitutional crisis from 1950 to 2019, every philosophical commitment of the document. It is structurally honest about how the document works, why it works that way, and where it has nearly broken.

What it does **not** narrate is the *institutional procedural detail* — the day-to-day mechanics. These are real PYQ targets and the spine does not, by itself, prepare the user for them.

**Final coverage estimate:**

| Stage | Coverage by topic | Coverage by PYQ-weight |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 (current) | 60-65% | 70-75% |
| After Phase 2 (nameplate dockings) | 75-80% | 85-88% |
| After supplementary procedure-and-mechanics surface | 90-93% | 95%+ |

The spine is not a complete polity preparation by itself — but it is, I think, a stronger conceptual foundation than any standard textbook treatment, and one that the procedural chapters can bolt onto cleanly.
