# Illustrations — Reading Guide

Before we talk about how the Earth became India, let's look at the shapes and forces that made it. These eight images sketch the core ideas: how continents move, how plates collide, how mountains rise, and how the deep structure beneath your feet explains everything you see above.

Each image is a window into one idea. Look for the patterns.

## The Story of Plates

![divergent-convergent-transform](/data/divergent-convergent-transform.webp)
*Where plates move apart, they create new crust. Where they collide, they destroy it or stack it. Where they slide past each other, they grind. All three happen in and around India.*

![earths-interior](/data/earths-interior.webp)
*The Earth is not a solid ball. It's layered: a thin crust you stand on, a thick mantle that flows, an outer core of liquid iron that moves, and an inner core of solid metal.*

## The Forces That Built India

![evolution-of-early-earth](/data/evolution-of-early-earth.webp)
*Four billion years ago, the Earth was different. Continents were elsewhere. The story of India is the story of how a fragment of Africa broke away, drifted east, and collided with Asia.*

![seismic-waves-magnetic-shield](/data/seismic-waves-magnetic-shield.webp)
*We know what's inside the Earth because earthquakes send waves through it. Those waves slow down, speed up, bend, and bounce as they hit different layers. A magnet surrounds us too, generated by the spinning liquid iron core.*

![supercontinent-cycle](/data/supercontinent-cycle.webp)
*Continents collide, stick together, then eventually break apart again. This dance takes 300 million years. India was once part of a giant continent called Gondwana. Then it broke free.*

![sprint-of-indian-plate1](/data/sprint-of-indian-plate1.webp)
*After India broke away from Africa and Gondwana, it moved fast—faster than other continental plates. For millions of years, it crossed an ocean toward Asia like a ship under sail.*

![sprint-of-indian-plate2](/data/sprint-of-indian-plate2.webp)
*When India hit Asia, the collision didn't stop. India kept pushing. The crust buckled. Mountains rose. That collision is still happening. The Himalayas grow a few millimeters every year.*

![subduction-mid-ocean-ridge](/data/subduction-mid-ocean-ridge.webp)
*At mid-ocean ridges, hot mantle wells up, cools, and spreads. At subduction zones, old, cold crust sinks back down. This recycling has been running for billions of years. It's how plates move. It's why India moved.*
