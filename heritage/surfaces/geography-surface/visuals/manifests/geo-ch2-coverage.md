## Concepts (in narrative order)

1. **frost-wedging** — Frost Wedging
   Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
   What: Freeze-thaw weathering: water in rock cracks expands ~9% on freezing, progressively fracturing rock; dominant weathering process at Himalayan altitudes.

2. **fold-mountains** — Fold Mountains
   Source: ncert — 6th_ch06_major_landforms_of_the_earth:geo_fold_mountains
   What: Mountains formed at convergent plate boundaries by compressional forces folding and thrusting crustal layers upward; Earth's highest ranges are fold mountains.

3. **alpine-himalayan-belt** — Alpine-Himalayan Belt
   Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
   What: Continuous mountain arc from Atlas Mountains (Morocco) through Alps, Caucasus, Zagros, Hindu Kush, Karakoram, Himalayas, bending south as Burmese arc into Indonesia.

4. **american-cordilleras** — American Cordilleras
   Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
   What: North-to-south mountain arc: Rockies (North America) + Andes (South America); formed by subduction along Pacific margin; world's longest continuous mountain chain.

5. **east-asian-arcs** — East Asian Island Arcs
   Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
   What: Pacific island arc system (Japan, Philippines, western Pacific); formed by oceanic-plate subduction under Asian margin, not continent-continent collision.

6. **himalayas** — Himalayas
   Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch02_structure_and_physiography:geo_himalayas
   What: Young fold mountain system running 2,400 km from Indus gorge (west) to Brahmaputra gorge (east); three to four parallel ridges stacked south to north; still tectonically active.

7. **trans-himalayas** — Trans-Himalayas
   Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
   What: Northernmost Himalayan zone (Karakoram, Ladakh ranges); absolute altitude peaks here (K2: 8,611 m); largest glacial ice mass in the system; inner side of India-Eurasia collision.

8. **greater-himalayas** — Greater Himalayas (Himadri)
   Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch02_structure_and_physiography:geo_greater_himalayas
   What: Main Himalayan watershed; crests rarely below 6,000 m; permanently snow-covered; holds all 8,000+ m peaks; composed of ancient upthrust Indian basement granite.

9. **sagarmatha** — Sagarmatha (Everest)
   Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
   What: World's highest peak at 8,849 m in Nepal Himalayas; also called Everest (English) and Chomolungma (Tibetan); part of Greater Himalayas.

10. **kanchenjunga** — Kanchenjunga
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: World's third-highest peak at 8,586 m; at Nepal-Sikkim junction; marks transition to wetter, narrower eastern Himalayan section.

11. **nanda-devi** — Nanda Devi
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: Highest summit entirely within India at 7,816 m; in Garhwal (Uttarakhand) Himalayas; within Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve.

12. **nanga-parbat** — Nanga Parbat (Western Syntaxis)
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: 8,126 m; western anchor of Greater Himalayas at the western syntaxis where the Himalayan arc bends sharply; known for vast south face relief.

13. **namcha-barwa** — Namcha Barwa (Eastern Syntaxis)
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: 7,782 m; eastern syntaxis point where Greater Himalayas bend abruptly south; beyond this, the range continues north-south as Purvanchal.

14. **lesser-himalayas** — Lesser Himalayas (Himachal)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch02_structure_and_physiography:geo_lesser_himalayas
    What: Second Himalayan zone south of Greater Himalayas; 3,700–4,500 m average altitude; location of major hill stations and three examiner-tested sub-ranges.

15. **pir-panjal** — Pir Panjal Range
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: Longest sub-range of Lesser Himalayas; runs through Jammu, Kashmir, and Himachal Pradesh; southern wall of the Kashmir Valley.

16. **dhauladhar** — Dhauladhar Range
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: Sub-range of Lesser Himalayas through Himachal Pradesh; one of three examiner-tested Lesser Himalayan sub-ranges (Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mahabharat).

17. **mahabharat-range** — Mahabharat Range
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: Sub-range of Lesser Himalayas through Nepal; one of three examiner-tested Lesser Himalayan sub-ranges; separates inner and outer Himalayan Nepal.

18. **kashmir-valley** — Kashmir Valley
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: Intermontane basin cradled between Pir Panjal (south) and Greater Himalayas (north); ~130 km × 40 km; one of world's most celebrated high-altitude structural basins.

19. **main-central-thrust** — Main Central Thrust (MCT)
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Major fault plane at the boundary between Greater and Lesser Himalayas; rock slabs thrust southward over each other; primary locus of Himalayan earthquakes.

20. **main-boundary-thrust** — Main Boundary Thrust (MBT)
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Major fault plane between Lesser Himalayas and Shivaliks; marks the active collision frontier; associated with ongoing seismic activity.

21. **shiwaliks** — Shivaliks (Outer Himalayas)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch02_structure_and_physiography:geo_shiwaliks
    What: Outermost, youngest Himalayan range; 900–1,100 m; made of unconsolidated river sediment folded by the advancing collision front; still sweeping southward.

22. **duns** — Duns (Longitudinal Valleys)
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: Flat-floored longitudinal valleys carved between Shivaliks and Lesser Himalayas; formed by erosion of softer rock between thrust ridges; Dehradun is the most famous example.

23. **himalayan-regional-sections** — Himalayan Regional Sections (West to East)
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: Himalayan arc divided into distinct regional sections: Punjab-Kashmir (widest, ~400 km, densely glaciated), Kumaon-Garhwal (Nanda Devi, Ganga glaciers), Nepal (Sagarmatha), Sikkim-Darjeeling (narrow, wet), Arunachal (eastern syntaxis).

24. **purvanchal** — Purvanchal (Eastern Highlands)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch02_structure_and_physiography:geo_purvanchal
    What: Himalayan fold system reoriented north-south beyond eastern syntaxis; Patkai, Naga, Manipur, Mizo hills; same collision origin as Himalayas, different orientation.

25. **burmese-arc** — Burmese Arc
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Continuation of the Alpine-Himalayan fold system running south through Myanmar and Indonesia; begins where Purvanchal bends past the eastern syntaxis.

26. **tethys-marine-fossils** — Tethys Marine Fossils at Altitude
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Marine fossils (ammonites) found at 4,000–5,000 m in Ladakh and Spiti; ancient Tethys sea-floor sediment compressed and uplifted during India-Eurasia collision; direct evidence of plate tectonics.

27. **third-pole** — The Third Pole
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Karakoram-Hindu Kush-Pamir-Himalaya cluster holds largest glacial ice mass outside Antarctica and Greenland; informally called the Third Pole; feeds major Asian river systems.

28. **siachen-glacier** — Siachen Glacier
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: ~76 km mountain glacier in Karakoram; among world's longest; source of Nubra river; holds strategic military significance (India-Pakistan).

29. **gangotri-glacier** — Gangotri Glacier
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: Major glacier in Garhwal (Uttarakhand); primary source of Ganga river system; retreating under climate change; located within Gangotri National Park.

30. **himalayan-glacial-meltwater** — Himalayan Glacial Meltwater Dependency
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Major rivers (Indus, Ganga, Yamuna, Brahmaputra) depend on glacial meltwater as a slow seasonal reservoir; agricultural calendars across Indo-Gangetic plains calibrated to melt rhythms.

31. **himalayan-seismic-hazard** — Himalayan Seismic Hazard
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Entire Himalayan front above active collision interface; Hindu Kush–Pamir knot among most seismically active zones globally; Bihar-Nepal 1934, Kashmir 2005, Nepal 2015 are landmark events.

32. **aravalli** — Aravalli Range
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch02_structure_and_physiography:geo_aravalli
    What: Fold mountains (~800 km, near Delhi to Gujarat); among world's oldest — Precambrian, 2–3 billion years; Guru Shikhar (1,722 m) is highest point; heavily eroded over eons.
