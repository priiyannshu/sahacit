## Concepts (in narrative order)

### Soil Science Foundations

1. **soil-four-components** — Soil as Four-Component System
   Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch06_soils:geo_india_soils
   What: Soil is simultaneously weathered rock, water, air, and life. Remove any one and you no longer have soil. All terrestrial agriculture rests on this meeting point of the four spheres.

2. **soil-formation-rate** — Soil Formation Rate
   Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
   What: ~1 cm per 1,000 years; typical productive soil ~1 metre depth = ~100,000 years of formation. Industrial agriculture can degrade that thickness in decades. The asymmetry between build-time and destroy-time is the central constraint of soil management.
   enhancement_target: true

3. **soil-formation-factors** — Five Factors of Soil Formation
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch06_geomorphic_processes:geo_soil_formation
   What: Parent material (granite → sandy acidic soils; basalt → heavy mineral-rich soils), climate (temperature + rainfall govern weathering rate and character), topography (slopes = thin coarse; valleys = thick fertile), biological activity (roots, decomposers, humus), time (deeper more developed profiles with age).

4. **soil-profile** — Soil Profile and Horizons
   Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch06_soils:geo_soil_profile
   What: O (organic), A (topsoil — humus + minerals), B (subsoil — leached-down minerals), C (weathered parent material), R (unweathered parent rock). Mature soil has all layers; young soils may have only nascent A over unweathered C.

5. **humus** — Humus
   Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch06_soils:geo_humus
   What: Organic soil component from decomposed plant/animal matter; dark brown/black; improves structure, water retention, aeration, and nutrients. Accumulates where decomposition is slower than input (temperate grassland); stripped away where decomposition is fast (tropical rainforest).

6. **leaching** — Leaching
   Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch06_soils:geo_leaching
   What: Soluble nutrients washed by rainwater into groundwater; most intense in heavy-rainfall tropical zones. Produces laterite soils (silica leached out, iron-Al oxides concentrated) and nutrient-poor latosols.

### World Soils

7. **chernozem** — Chernozem (Black Earth)
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch15_life_on_the_earth:geo_temperate_grassland
   What: Deep humus-rich black earths of Eurasian steppes and North American prairies; most productive agricultural soils on Earth. Organic matter accumulates faster than it decomposes under temperate grassland. Wheat belts of Russia, Ukraine, Canada sit on chernozems.

8. **latosol** — Latosol (Tropical Forest Soil)
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch15_life_on_the_earth:geo_tropical_rainforest_biome
   What: Tropical rainforest soils (Amazon, Congo, Indo-Malayan arc) — appear rich but are nutrient-poor; fast decomposition prevents humus accumulation; leaching strips topsoil. Nutrients locked in living biomass, not soil. Clearing the forest exhausts soil within seasons.

9. **podzol** — Podzol (Taiga Soil)
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch15_life_on_the_earth:geo_taiga_biome
   What: Strongly acidic, heavily leached, grey soils under cold coniferous taiga (Russia, Canada). Low nutrient availability; poor agriculture. Characterises the boreal forest belt.

### India's Soils

10. **alluvial-soil** — Alluvial Soil
    Source: ncert — 10th_ch01_resources_and_development:geo_alluvial_soil
    What: Most extensive in India (~40% land area); entire Indo-Gangetic plains + coastal deltas. River-deposited silt. Rich in potash, poor in phosphorus and nitrogen. Grows rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute, oilseeds — the whole agricultural heartland.

11. **bhangar** — Bhangar (Old Alluvium)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch06_soils:geo_bhangar
    What: Older alluvium above active floodplain; slightly elevated, compact, dark; contains kankar (calcium carbonate nodules). Less fertile. Historically preferred for settlement (flood safety).

12. **khadar** — Khadar (New Alluvium)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch06_soils:geo_khadar
    What: Younger alluvium deposited in active floodplain; renewed with fresh silt every flood season; lighter, sandier, more fertile. Historically preferred for farming despite flood risk.

13. **black-soil** — Black Soil (Regur / Black Cotton Soil)
    Source: ncert — 10th_ch01_resources_and_development:geo_black_soil
    What: Weathered Deccan Trap basalt (66 Ma); Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat, Karnataka, AP. Clay-heavy, rich in iron/lime/calcium/magnesium. Self-ploughing (swells wet, cracks dry). Retains moisture far longer than any other Indian type — ideal for cotton. Poor in nitrogen and phosphorus.

14. **red-yellow-soil** — Red and Yellow Soil
    Source: ncert — 10th_ch01_resources_and_development:geo_red_yellow_soil
    What: Develops on ancient crystalline/metamorphic rock (granite, gneiss) of the Gondwana shield; peninsular fringes — TN, Karnataka, AP, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Bundelkhand. Red from iron oxide; yellow when iron is hydrated. Porous, low in nitrogen and humus; responds to fertiliser and irrigation. Supports millets, groundnut, pulses.

15. **laterite-soil** — Laterite Soil
    Source: ncert — 10th_ch01_resources_and_development:geo_laterite_soil
    What: High rainfall + dry season → intense leaching removes silica, concentrating iron-Al oxides. Hard when dry, soft when wet, poor in plant nutrients. Western Ghats windward slopes, Eastern Ghats, Kerala, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Meghalaya. Historically quarried as building stone. Supports cashew, tea, coffee, rubber with significant inputs.

16. **arid-soil** — Arid/Desert Soil
    Source: ncert — 10th_ch01_resources_and_development:geo_arid_soil
    What: Sandy, low organic matter, high salt from high evaporation; kankar layer blocks water infiltration. Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana. Productive with irrigation (Indira Gandhi Canal) but salinisation risk constant.

17. **saline-alkaline-soil** — Saline and Alkaline Soil (Reh/Usar/Kallar)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch06_soils:geo_saline_soil
    What: High salt from poor drainage, high evaporation, or waterlogging. Coastal soils (seawater). Critically spreading in irrigated plains of Punjab, Haryana, UP where canal irrigation without drainage has raised water tables, drawing salts to surface by capillary action. White crust; hostile to crops. Reclaimed slowly with gypsum and drainage.

18. **peaty-soil** — Peaty/Marshy Soil
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch06_soils:geo_peaty_soil
    What: High organic matter, very acidic, poorly aerated; waterlogged high-humidity environments. Kerala backwaters (especially Kuttanad — farming below sea level), parts of Bengal, coastal Odisha, Sundarbans fringe. Supports rice when drained.

19. **forest-mountain-soil** — Forest and Mountain Soil
    Source: ncert — 10th_ch01_resources_and_development:geo_forest_soil
    What: Himalayan slopes, parts of Western and Eastern Ghats; heterogeneous — thin and acidic on steep faces, thicker and richer in valley pockets. Changes character with altitude. Supports Darjeeling and Assam tea, HP orchards.

### Soil Erosion and Conservation

20. **chambal-ravines** — Chambal Ravines and Gully Erosion
    Source: ncert — 10th_ch01_resources_and_development:geo_soil_erosion_10
    What: Overgrazing + vegetation removal → rainfall cut channels → gullies → ravines across MP and Rajasthan. Textbook gully erosion; tens of thousands of sq km converted to badland, unrecoverable. India loses ~5 billion tonnes of topsoil annually to erosion.

21. **soil-conservation-methods** — Soil Conservation Methods
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch06_soils:geo_soil_conservation_india
    What: Terracing (flat steps on hill slopes), contour bunding (earthen barriers along contours), strip cropping, shelterbelts (tree rows in arid/semi-arid zones), watershed management at catchment scale. Toolkit is well-established; the missing ingredient is sustained land-use discipline.

### World Biomes

22. **world-biomes** — World Biomes Concept
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch15_life_on_the_earth:geo_biome
    What: Large-scale ecological communities adapted to a climatic region. Biome map almost exactly overlays the Köppen climate classification — the same climate system described in ch5 now showing up as a vegetation map.

23. **tropical-rainforest-biome** — Tropical Rainforest Biome
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch15_life_on_the_earth:geo_tropical_rainforest_biome
    What: Equatorial; >2000 mm rain, no dry season; most biodiverse; multi-layered canopy. Amazon, Congo, Indo-Malayan arc. Latosol soils — nutrients locked in living biomass, not soil.

24. **tropical-savanna-biome** — Tropical Savanna Biome
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch15_life_on_the_earth:geo_savanna_biome
    What: Between forest and desert; distinct wet and dry seasons; grasses with scattered fire-resistant trees. East African savannas, Llanos (Venezuela), Cerrado (Brazil), Campos (Brazil).

25. **desert-biome** — Desert Biome and Vegetation
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch15_life_on_the_earth:geo_desert_biome
    What: <250 mm rainfall; convergent plant adaptations across Sahara, Thar, Atacama, Kalahari — deep roots, small waxy leaves, water storage, dormancy. Desert soils low in organic matter, often with calcium carbonate layer near surface.

26. **mediterranean-vegetation** — Mediterranean Vegetation
    Source: ncert — 7th_ch06_natural_vegetation_and_wildlife:geo_mediterranean_veg
    What: W and SW continental margins; hot dry summers, mild rainy winters. Hard-leaved, fire-adapted shrubs. Thick bark, wax-coated leaves. 'Orchards of the World' for citrus. Mediterranean Sea rim, California coast, central Chile, SW Australia.

27. **temperate-grassland-biome** — Temperate Grasslands — Global Names
    Source: ncert — 7th_ch06_natural_vegetation_and_wildlife:geo_grassland_names
    What: Mid-latitude grasslands on chernozem soils — Prairies (North America), Pampas (Argentina/Uruguay), Steppes (Eurasia), Veld (South Africa), Downs (Australia). Heavily converted to grain agriculture. Wheat belts of Russia, Ukraine, Canada.

28. **temperate-deciduous-forest** — Temperate Deciduous Forest
    Source: ncert — 7th_ch06_natural_vegetation_and_wildlife:geo_temp_deciduous
    What: Oaks, beeches, maples; Europe, eastern USA, East Asia. Shed leaves in dry/cold season. Between Mediterranean and boreal.

29. **taiga-boreal-forest** — Taiga (Boreal Forest)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch15_life_on_the_earth:geo_taiga_biome
    What: Largest terrestrial biome; 50-70°N across Russia and Canada. Coniferous trees (spruce, pine, fir) on podzol soils; minimal understory. Long harsh winters, short cool summers.

30. **tundra-biome** — Tundra Biome
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch15_life_on_the_earth:geo_tundra_biome
    What: Polar regions; permafrost; mosses, lichens, grasses, dwarf shrubs. Very short growing season (6-10 weeks). No trees. Approaches permanent ice.

### India's Vegetation Types

31. **india-vegetation-overview** — India's Vegetation Diversity
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch05_natural_vegetation:geo_india_vegetation
    What: India's latitude range + Himalayan altitude gradient compresses several global biome types into one national territory. Vegetation sorted by rainfall: >200 cm (evergreen), 100-200 cm (moist deciduous), 70-100 cm (dry deciduous), <70 cm (thorn), plus montane zonation and coastal mangroves.

32. **tropical-evergreen-forest** — Tropical Evergreen Forest (India)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch05_natural_vegetation:geo_tropical_evergreen_forest
    What: >200 cm rainfall, no significant dry season. Western Ghats windward slopes, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, northeastern hills (Meghalaya, Arunachal, Assam hills, Nagaland). Multi-layered; perpetually green (species shed at different times). Rosewood, mahogany, ebony, bamboo. High endemism.

33. **shola-forests** — Shola Forests
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch05_natural_vegetation:geo_shola_forest
    What: Unique tropical montane evergreen patches in valley floors of Western Ghats above 1,500 m (Nilgiri, Anaimalai, Palani Hills), interspersed with grassland on hilltops. Stunted evergreen canopy. Among India's most endemic and fragile ecosystems.

34. **moist-deciduous-forest** — Moist Deciduous Forest (India)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch05_natural_vegetation:geo_moist_deciduous
    What: 100-200 cm rainfall; leaves shed for 6-8 weeks. Eastern slopes of Western Ghats, northeastern Himalayan foothills, Jharkhand, Odisha. Teak, sal, shisham, mahua, bamboo. Heart of India's timber economy.

35. **dry-deciduous-forest** — Dry Deciduous Forest (India)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch05_natural_vegetation:geo_dry_deciduous
    What: 70-100 cm rainfall; open canopy, longer dry season. MP, Maharashtra interior, UP, drier peninsula. Peepal, neem, sheesham, dry bamboo. With moist deciduous, forms the core of commercial forestry — teak and sal are the organising species.

36. **thorn-forest** — Tropical Thorn Forest (India)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch05_natural_vegetation:geo_thorn_forest
    What: <70 cm rainfall; Rajasthan, Saurashtra, parts of MP and southern Punjab. Stunted, thorned acacia (babul), khejri, wild date palm. Small waxy leaves, thick bark, deep roots — all solutions to the same problem: water is scarce and soil holds almost none.

37. **bishnoi-khejri** — Bishnoi Community and Khejri
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid-india)
    What: Bishnoi community of Rajasthan has organised an ethic of wildlife and forest protection specifically around khejri (Prosopis cineraria) — the most drought-resistant tree of the Thar, providing fodder, fuel, and nitrogen. Opposition to tree-felling historically documented from the 18th century.
    enhancement_target: true

38. **montane-forest** — Montane Forest — Himalayan Zonation
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch05_natural_vegetation:geo_montane_forest
    What: Altitude compresses latitudinal biome zones: subtropical broadleaf (foothills) → wet temperate oak/chestnut (1,000-2,000 m) → temperate coniferous deodar/blue pine/fir/spruce (2,000-3,000 m) → alpine rhododendron scrub (3,000-4,000 m) → mosses/lichens → permanent snow. Darjeeling tea in lower-middle belt; HP orchards in coniferous zone.

39. **mangrove-forest** — Mangrove Forest — Sundarbans and Coasts
    Source: ncert — 11th_p2_ch05_natural_vegetation:geo_mangrove_forest
    What: Tidal coastal forests. Sundarbans (Ganges-Brahmaputra delta) — largest single mangrove tract in the world; Sundari tree (Heritiera fomes); last significant Bengal tiger coastal habitat. Also: Bhitarkanika (Odisha), Pichavaram (Tamil Nadu), Gulf of Kachchh, Andaman and Nicobar. Deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery.

40. **mangrove-adaptations-and-role** — Mangrove Adaptations and Ecological Role
    Source: writer-ai
    What: Structural adaptations: pneumatophores (breathing roots projecting above waterlogged mud), stilt roots (anchor in shifting tidal substrate), viviparous seeds (germinate on the tree). Ecological role: cyclone storm-surge buffer, sediment trap, fish nursery. 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami showed measurably less damage where intact mangroves remained.
    enhancement_target: true

41. **species-habitat-dependency** — Species-Habitat Dependency
    Source: writer-ai
    What: Each flagship species depends on one specific vegetation community: Sundarbans tiger → coastal mangrove; Western Ghats lion-tailed macaque → multi-layered evergreen forest; Kaziranga one-horned rhinoceros → Brahmaputra floodplain grassland (seasonal flooding, large undisturbed tract). When that community shrinks, the species has nowhere else to go.
    enhancement_target: true
