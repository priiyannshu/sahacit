## Concepts (in narrative order)

1. **big-bang-theory** — Big Bang Theory
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_big_bang_theory
   What: Universe expanded from a singularity ~13.7 billion years ago; expansion continues today; cosmic background radiation is observational evidence.

2. **nebular-hypothesis** — Nebular Hypothesis
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_nebular_hypothesis
   What: Solar system formed from a rotating cloud of gas and dust contracting under gravity; sun at centre, planets from leftover disc.

3. **star-formation** — Star Formation (Sun ignition)
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_star_formation
   What: Stars form when gas in a nebula contracts until core temperature triggers hydrogen fusion; Sun ignited ~4.6 Ga.

4. **solar-system** — Solar System
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_solar_system
   What: Sun + 8 planets + satellites + asteroids + comets + meteoroids; formed ~4.6 Ga from solar nebula.

5. **terrestrial-planets** — Terrestrial Planets
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_terrestrial_planets
   What: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars — small, dense, rocky, formed close to Sun where heat boiled off lighter material.

6. **jovian-planets** — Jovian Planets
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_jovian_planets
   What: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune — large, gaseous, formed far from Sun where lighter gases survived.

7. **earth-goldilocks-zone** — Earth's Habitable Position
   Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
   What: Earth's distance from Sun places it in the narrow band where water stays liquid long-term; precondition for life and oceans.

8. **earth-differentiation** — Differentiation of Earth
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_differentiation
   What: Density-driven separation of early molten Earth — heavy iron/nickel sank to form core, lighter silicates rose to form crust.

9. **moon-formation** — Moon Formation (Giant Impact)
   Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_moon_formation
   What: Mars-sized body Theia collided with proto-Earth ~4.5 Ga; ejected debris accreted into Moon; collision set Earth's 23.5° axial tilt.

10. **degassing** — Degassing
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_degassing
    What: Release of trapped gases from Earth's molten interior through volcanic vents; produced primitive atmosphere of water vapour, CO₂, methane, ammonia.

11. **origin-of-atmosphere** — Origin of Atmosphere
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_origin_of_atmosphere
    What: First atmosphere of H/He lost to solar wind; second from volcanic degassing (H₂O, CO₂); modern oxygen-rich atmosphere from photosynthesis.

12. **origin-of-oceans** — Origin of Oceans
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_origin_of_oceans
    What: As Earth cooled, atmospheric water vapour condensed and rained for a long period, filling low-lying basins to form oceans.

13. **origin-of-life** — Origin of Life
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch02_origin_and_evolution_of_the_earth:geo_origin_of_life
    What: Life originated ~3.8 Ga in oceans; first organisms unicellular prokaryotes; cyanobacteria began oxygen photosynthesis ~2.5 Ga.

14. **earths-crust** — Earth's Crust
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_earths_crust
    What: Outermost solid layer; continental crust 30–35 km thick (up to 70 km under mountains), oceanic crust 5–7 km thick.

15. **sial** — Sial (Continental Crust)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_sial
    What: Upper continental crust dominated by silica and aluminium; granitic; density ~2.7 g/cm³.

16. **sima** — Sima (Oceanic Crust)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_sima
    What: Lower/oceanic crust dominated by silica and magnesium; basaltic; density ~3.0 g/cm³, denser than sial.

17. **mantle** — Mantle
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_mantle
    What: Layer between crust and core, ~35 km to 2,900 km depth; silicate rock rich in iron and magnesium; mostly solid, partially molten in upper reaches.

18. **earths-core** — Earth's Core
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_core
    What: Innermost layer 2,900–6,371 km; outer core liquid iron-nickel (generates magnetic field via dynamo), inner core solid iron-nickel under colossal pressure.

19. **earth-magnetic-field** — Earth's Magnetic Field
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Magnetosphere generated by dynamo action of liquid outer core; deflects solar wind and shields surface from lethal radiation; precondition for atmosphere retention.

20. **p-waves** — P-Waves (Primary)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_p_waves
    What: Fastest seismic waves; longitudinal/compressional; travel through solids, liquids, and gases.

21. **s-waves** — S-Waves (Secondary)
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_s_waves
    What: Transverse shear seismic waves; cannot travel through liquids; absence creates the shadow zone, evidencing liquid outer core.

22. **shadow-zone** — Shadow Zone
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_shadow_zone
    What: Region 104°–140° from earthquake epicentre where seismic waves are not directly detected; due to refraction by liquid outer core.

23. **moho-discontinuity** — Mohorovicic Discontinuity
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_moho_discontinuity
    What: Boundary between crust and mantle; ~35 km under continents, 5–8 km under oceans; discovered by Andrija Mohorovicic, 1909.

24. **gutenberg-discontinuity** — Gutenberg Discontinuity
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_gutenberg_discontinuity
    What: Boundary between mantle and outer core at ~2,900 km depth; S-waves disappear here, indicating transition to liquid; named after Beno Gutenberg.

25. **lehmann-discontinuity** — Lehmann Discontinuity
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Boundary between liquid outer core and solid inner core at ~5,150 km depth; named after Inge Lehmann (1936).

26. **lithosphere** — Lithosphere
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_lithosphere
    What: Rigid outermost shell comprising crust + uppermost solid mantle; ~100 km thick; broken into tectonic plates that float on the asthenosphere.

27. **asthenosphere** — Asthenosphere
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch03_interior_of_the_earth:geo_asthenosphere
    What: Zone in upper mantle ~100–410 km depth; partially molten, weak, plastic; convection currents here drive plate movement.

28. **mantle-convection-currents** — Mantle Convection Currents
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_convection_currents
    What: Circular movement of mantle material driven by interior heat; hot rises, moves laterally beneath lithosphere, cools, sinks; engine of plate motion.

29. **major-tectonic-plates** — Major Tectonic Plates
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_major_plates
    What: Seven major lithospheric plates — Pacific (largest), North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian, Antarctic; plus minor plates (Nazca, Philippine, Arabian, Caribbean, etc.).

30. **divergent-boundary** — Divergent Boundary
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_divergent_boundary
    What: Plate boundary where plates move apart; magma rises from asthenosphere creating new crust; produces mid-ocean ridges and rift valleys.

31. **convergent-boundary** — Convergent Boundary
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_convergent_boundary
    What: Plate boundary where plates collide; three types — oceanic-oceanic (island arcs), oceanic-continental (subduction trenches + volcanic mountains), continental-continental (fold mountains).

32. **transform-boundary** — Transform Boundary
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_transform_boundary
    What: Plate boundary where plates slide past each other horizontally; no crust created or destroyed; causes earthquakes but no volcanism (e.g., San Andreas Fault).

33. **subduction** — Subduction
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_subduction
    What: Process at convergent boundaries where denser oceanic plate sinks beneath another plate into the mantle; creates trenches and intense volcanism.

34. **mid-ocean-ridge** — Mid-Ocean Ridge
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_mid_ocean_ridge
    What: Underwater mountain chain at divergent boundaries; longest mountain range on Earth (~65,000 km); Mid-Atlantic Ridge most prominent example.

35. **ring-of-fire** — Ring of Fire
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_ring_of_fire
    What: Horseshoe-shaped zone around Pacific Ocean with intense seismic and volcanic activity; ~75% of world's volcanoes and ~90% of earthquakes.

36. **continental-drift** — Continental Drift Theory
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_continental_drift
    What: Wegener's 1912 theory that all continents were once joined as Pangaea and drifted apart; evidence from coastline match, fossils, geological formations.

37. **wegener** — Alfred Wegener
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_wegener
    What: German meteorologist who proposed continental drift in 1912; could not explain mechanism, so theory was rejected for half a century before being vindicated.

38. **pangaea** — Pangaea
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_pangaea
    What: Supercontinent ~250 Ma comprising all present-day continents; began breaking apart in Triassic; first split into Laurasia (north) and Gondwana (south).

39. **panthalassa** — Panthalassa
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: Single global ocean surrounding Pangaea before continental breakup; "all sea".

40. **laurasia** — Laurasia
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_laurasia
    What: Northern supercontinent from Pangaea split; comprised present-day North America, Europe, Asia; separated from Gondwana by Tethys Sea.

41. **gondwana** — Gondwana
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_gondwana
    What: Southern supercontinent comprising South America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica; named after the Gondwana region of India where matching Glossopteris fossils were found.

42. **tethys-sea** — Tethys Sea
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_tethys_sea
    What: Ancient shallow sea between Laurasia and Gondwana; accumulated thick sediments over millions of years; sediments later folded and uplifted to form the Himalayas.

43. **arthur-holmes-convection-mechanism** — Arthur Holmes' Convection Proposal
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: 1930s proposal by Arthur Holmes that mantle convection currents could be the physical force driving continental drift; supplied the mechanism Wegener lacked.

44. **harry-hess-seafloor-spreading** — Harry Hess and Sea Floor Spreading
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_seafloor_spreading
    What: 1960s discovery by Harry Hess that ocean floor is being newly created at mid-ocean ridges and spreading outward; magnetic stripes in basalt confirmed symmetric formation.

45. **plate-tectonics** — Plate Tectonics Theory
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_plate_tectonics
    What: Modern theory that Earth's lithosphere is divided into rigid plates moving on the asthenosphere; absorbs continental drift; explains drift, mountain-building, earthquakes, volcanism.

46. **indian-plate** — Indian Plate
    Source: ncert — 11th_p1_ch04_distribution_of_oceans_and_continents:geo_indian_plate
    What: Tectonic plate carrying the Indian subcontinent; broke from Gondwana ~140 Ma, sprinted north at up to 15 cm/yr, collided with Eurasian plate ~50 Ma; still moving north at ~5 cm/yr.

47. **india-himalayan-collision** — India-Eurasia Collision and Himalayan Uplift
    Source: writer-ai (pending: leong)
    What: ~50 Ma collision of northward-drifting Indian plate with Eurasian plate; Tethys sediments compressed and lifted upward, forming Himalayas; uplift continues at ~5 mm/yr today.

48. **india-three-zone-inheritance** — India's Three-Zone Geological Inheritance
    Source: writer-ai (pending: majid)
    What: Synthesis framing — India is an assemblage of three geological inheritances: ancient Gondwanan peninsular shield (south), young Himalayan front (north), and Indo-Gangetic alluvial plain laid by rivers between them.
