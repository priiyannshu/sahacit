# Maths Surface — Vision

## The Problem This Surface Solves

Mathematics was taught wrong. Not wrong in content — wrong in entry point. Symbols and procedures were handed to a human brain before that brain had any sense of *what problem these symbols were invented to solve* or *what reality they describe*. That produced reinforced aversion, not understanding.

The reinvention: treat mathematics as a **reality interpretation language** — the set of tools humans built to describe shape, change, structure, quantity, uncertainty, and relationship in the world. Every branch came into existence because someone needed to describe something real and the existing language couldn't do it. That is the frame.

## What This Surface Is For

1. **Mapping the territory** — getting the whole shape of mathematics in mind: what branches exist, what each one is *about*, how they connect to each other and to physical reality. Not mastery, not yet — geography first.

2. **Concept-first, symbol-second** — every node opens with the intuition: *what is this describing?* Symbols follow only after the idea lands. No formula before the question it answers.

3. **Visual reality interpretation** — geometry isn't about proofs, it's about space. Analysis isn't about epsilons, it's about change. The surface foregrounds the *phenomenon* each area of math is trying to describe.

4. **UPSC Mains Optional track** — the Maths optional (Paper 1 + Paper 2) covers a specific syllabus. This surface should make that territory navigable without losing the bigger picture. The optional is a *use case*, not the entire purpose.

5. **Personal, not encyclopedic** — built for one person's specific learning style, aversions, and gaps. This is not a textbook. It's a cognition prosthetic.

## What Makes This Different From Other Surfaces

- **History/Polity/Economics** surfaces are narrative — they tell a story and the user reads/listens.
- **Maths surface** is *interactive* — you can't just narrate calculus and call it done. Understanding lands through manipulation: sketch a curve, watch a limit approach, rotate a shape, play with a parameter.

So the maths surface is the first PWA surface where **embedded tools are load-bearing**, not decorative. Visualization widgets, interactive proofs, a graphing layer — these aren't extras, they're the core of the surface.

## The UPSC Maths Optional Syllabus (Reference)

**Paper 1:**
- Linear Algebra (vector spaces, matrices, eigenvalues)
- Calculus (limits, continuity, differentiation, integration, multiple integrals)
- Analytic Geometry (2D/3D: conics, planes, spheres, conicoids)
- Ordinary Differential Equations
- Dynamics (rigid body, Lagrangian mechanics basics)
- Statics
- Vector Analysis

**Paper 2:**
- Algebra (groups, rings, fields, Galois theory intro)
- Real Analysis (metric spaces, sequences, continuity, differentiation, Riemann/Lebesgue integration)
- Complex Analysis (analytic functions, conformal mapping, residues)
- Linear Programming (simplex, duality, assignment)
- Partial Differential Equations
- Numerical Analysis
- Mechanics and Fluid Dynamics

## What Comes Next

This is not a spec. The surface will be built one node, one branch, one experiment at a time. The workflow (narrative → visual → interactive) will be discovered through doing, the same way history-surface and polity-surface were. This folder holds the foundation; the surface evolves through sessions.
